Privacy Policy — Auto Expense Tracker

Last updated: October 31, 2026

Auto Expense Tracker ("the app") is an Android app that helps you track your spending automatically. This page explains what information the app accesses, what it does with it, and what it never does. We wrote this to be accurate and specific to how the app actually works, not generic boilerplate. This policy is kept in sync with the Data Safety declarations we file in Google Play Console — the two describe the same facts, so nothing here should contradict what's declared there.

The short version

Your financial transaction data is processed and stored only on your device, encrypted. We do not operate a server, and your transaction history is never uploaded anywhere, ever. The only network activity the app performs is talking to Google's own services — for ads shown to free users, the optional Pro subscription, and update and review prompts — none of which receive your financial transaction data.

What the app reads

With your permission (Android's "Notification Access" setting, which you must explicitly grant and can revoke at any time), the app reads the text of notifications from your banking, UPI, and default messaging apps, specifically to detect transaction alerts (e.g. "Rs.500 debited...", "You received Rs.1200...").

When you first turn Notification Access on, the app also looks once at the bank and UPI alerts that are already sitting in your notification shade, using exactly the same filter and the same extraction as for a new alert, so that your first screen is not empty. It does not read your older notifications or messages beyond what is currently in the shade, and it does not do this again on later reconnects.

Banks send these alerts from a registered sender code (e.g. "HDFCBK"), not a normal app or contact name. To detect alerts from banks that aren't on our known-app list, the app also checks the sender name of notifications from apps outside that list against the pattern used by these registered bank codes. Only the sender name is checked for such apps — the message text itself is not read unless the sender name matches this bank-code pattern. If it doesn't match, nothing about that notification is stored, logged, or leaves your device. The app does not read notification content from social media, chat apps, or anything unrelated to financial transaction alerts.

From a matching notification, the app extracts only:

  • The transaction amount
  • Whether it was money sent or received
  • The date/time
  • The counterparty name or account, where present in the message
  • The account balance, where the message happens to include one

The original notification text itself is discarded immediately after these details are extracted. It is never saved to disk, never logged in the version of the app you download from Google Play, and never leaves your device. The one thing kept that is derived from it is a one-way fingerprint (a SHA-256 hash), used only so the same alert is not recorded twice.

The app does not use SMS permissions of any kind. It never requests READ_SMS or RECEIVE_SMS. Notification Access is the only mechanism used to detect transactions.

What the app stores

The structured details above (amount, direction, date, counterparty, category, your own notes/tags) are stored in an encrypted database on your device, using industry-standard encryption (SQLCipher) with a key held in your device's own secure hardware (Android Keystore) where available. This data is never transmitted off your device — there is no backend server for this app to send it to. Android's automatic cloud backup and device-to-device transfer are turned off for this app, so your data is not copied to your Google account or to a new phone behind your back.

The app also keeps, on your device only, the things you set up or do in it: your budgets, bill reminders, balances you typed in by hand, the category you chose for a merchant ("always use this category for this merchant"), your recent searches, your display and notification preferences, and a small counter of how many times this month you have used a free export or import (see below).

The optional home-screen widget shows this month's totals from that same on-device data. Anyone who can see your home screen can see the widget, so remove it if that matters to you.

Exports, backups and imports

You can export your data at any time: a JSON backup, a CSV file for spreadsheets, or a PDF report for a day, month or year. You choose where the file is saved, using Android's own file picker. This is an action you take deliberately; the app does not do it automatically or without your action.

These exported files are not encrypted. Anyone who can open the file can read it, so save it somewhere you trust and delete it when you no longer need it. Likewise you can import a backup or a CSV file (including a bank statement CSV) by choosing the file yourself; it is read on your device and not uploaded anywhere.

You can permanently delete all app data at any time from Settings. This erases your transactions, budgets, reminders, hand-entered balances, category rules, recent searches and related settings from the app. Files you exported yourself stay where you saved them until you delete them.

What we never do

  • We do not operate a server that receives your transaction data.
  • We do not sell, share, or transmit your financial transaction data to any third party.
  • We do not use your transaction data for advertising targeting.
  • We do not read notifications from apps unrelated to financial transactions.

Data that Google's own services process

The app uses a small number of Google services, which is the only network access the app has. This is separate from, and does not involve, your financial transaction data:

  • Google AdMob (for ads shown to free users: a banner at the bottom of the screen, a card on the Search screen, and optional short video ads you can choose to watch to unlock a free export or import): the Google Mobile Ads SDK sends data to Google to serve and measure ads and to prevent fraud. According to Google's own disclosure for this SDK, that includes your device's IP address (which can be used to estimate your general location), how you interact with the ads and the app (such as app launches, taps and video views), diagnostic information about the app's performance, and device identifiers such as the Advertising ID and an app-set ID. This is subject to your ad-consent choice (users in the EEA and UK are shown a consent form on first launch, per Google's UMP requirements, and everyone can change this choice any time from Settings → Privacy & Ad Consent) and to the Advertising ID controls in your Android settings. Your transaction data is never part of this. Pro subscribers see no ads.
  • Google Play Billing (for the optional Pro subscription): processes your purchase history for this app's subscription only. Payment details (card numbers, UPI IDs, etc.) are handled entirely by Google Play and never reach this app or its developer — we only ever see a subscription status (active/expired), not payment instrument details.
  • Google Play's in-app update and in-app review features: check for app updates and, occasionally, prompt for a Play Store rating. Standard Google Play services, not custom infrastructure of ours.

See Google's own Privacy Policy for how these Google services handle data: https://policies.google.com/privacy

Security practices

  • Encryption at rest: your transaction data is encrypted on-device (SQLCipher/AES-256) with a key held in Android Keystore hardware where available. Files you export are not encrypted (see above).
  • Encryption in transit: not applicable to your financial data — it is never transmitted anywhere. The only network traffic this app generates is the Google services above, which use Google's own encrypted connections.
  • Data deletion: there is no server-side account to delete, because there is no server. You can permanently erase all locally stored data at any time from Settings → Delete All Data, which takes effect immediately and cannot be undone. Uninstalling the app also removes the data the app stores, since nothing is stored anywhere else; files you exported yourself are the exception, as they are saved where you chose.

Permissions the app uses, and why

  • Notification access — to detect bank/UPI transaction alerts, as described above.
  • Notifications (POST_NOTIFICATIONS) — to show you the notifications the app offers: bill reminders, budget warnings, unusual-spending alerts, subscription price-change alerts and, only if you turn them on, a daily check-in and a weekly summary.
  • Ignore battery optimizations (request only, never silent) — the app can ask to be exempted from aggressive battery management, so the notification listener isn't killed in the background and stops capturing transactions. You choose whether to grant this from a dedicated settings screen; nothing happens without you tapping through it.
  • Biometric/fingerprint — only if you turn on the optional app lock feature, to unlock the app.
  • Internet & network state — used only for the Google services described above; never for transmitting your transaction data.

Children's privacy

This app is a personal finance utility intended for general adult use and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect information from children.

Your choices

  • You can grant or revoke Notification Access at any time from Android Settings.
  • You can change your ad-consent choice at any time from the app's Settings screen, and reset or limit your Advertising ID in Android Settings.
  • You can export or permanently delete all your data at any time from the app's Settings screen.
  • You can switch the daily check-in and weekly summary notifications on or off in the app's Settings screen.
